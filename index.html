How it plays

Fill all 36 squares with the six chess pieces. Each row, each column and each 2×3 box holds all six exactly once — that is the sudoku half. The chess half: no piece may see another of its own kind. Sight ignores whatever stands in the way, so a bishop rules its whole diagonal. Different pieces never mind each other — a queen is happy beside a queen's worth of knights, just not beside another queen.

Tap a square, then tap a piece below the board to place it — two taps, every time. The square stays selected, so tap a different piece to swap it, or tap the same piece again to clear it. On a keyboard you can also press P N B R Q K or 1–6; arrows move; Backspace clears.

A finding from the solver, worth knowing while you play. Because rows and columns already forbid a repeat, the rook's sight adds nothing at all — it is the free piece. For the same reason the queen ends up with exactly the bishop's restriction (no twin on a diagonal), and the king ends up with exactly the pawn's (no twin diagonally touching). Three real rules do the work: diagonal touch, diagonal line, knight's leap.
